Take a moment to consider the risks before starting a job. Good preparation prevents incidents.

Checkpoints
👤 1. Am I ready?
Instructions & procedures
Do I know the work instructions and procedures for this task?
Competence & training
Am I authorized and trained to perform this work?
Physical condition
Do I feel fit enough? No influence from medication, alcohol or fatigue?
🛡️ 2. Are the right precautions in place?
Work permit
Is there a valid work permit (if required)?
PPE available
Do I have the correct personal protective equipment and am I using it?
Tools & materials
Are tools suitable and in good condition? Correct materials available?
Safety provisions
Are safety provisions (barriers, lockout, ventilation) in order?
🔍 3. Have I checked the workplace?
Environmental hazards
Are there hazards in the environment? (traffic, other work, weather)
Fall & trip hazards
Is the workplace free from fall, trip and slip hazards?
Explosion & toxic hazards
Is the atmosphere safe? Are there risks from gas, vapor or oxygen deficiency?
Escape routes & emergency facilities
Do I know the escape routes? Do I know where the safety shower, eyewash and fire extinguisher are?
🤝 4. Communication & collaboration
Colleagues informed
Do colleagues and/or the control room know what I am going to do?
Communication devices
Do I have working communication devices (radio, phone)?

Dirty Dozen Check

Recognize the 12 human factors that lead to errors.

Why this check?
Recognize risk factors before they lead to errors
The "Dirty Dozen" was developed in the aviation industry and identifies 12 human factors that cause most errors and incidents. Research shows that 80% of all incidents have a human cause.

By recognizing these factors before starting work, you can consciously take action to prevent errors. Be honest in your self-reflection — it's not about scoring perfectly, but about awareness of risks that can affect your alertness and performance.

Click each item: 1× = ⚠️ Attention point (read the tip) → 2× = ✓ OK → 3× = ✗ Risk
The 12 Factors
1. Lack of Communication
Poor Communication
Have I received and understood all necessary information? Have I communicated clearly myself?
💡 Tip: Use 3-way communication: say, repeat, confirm. Ask questions when in doubt.
2. Complacency
Complacency
Am I alert or working on autopilot? Do I still take routine tasks seriously?
💡 Tip: Expect the unexpected. Consciously check every step, even with routine tasks.
3. Lack of Knowledge
Lack of Knowledge
Do I have sufficient knowledge and training for this task? Do I know where to find information?
💡 Tip: Ask for help or clarification when in doubt. Consult procedures and documentation.
4. Distraction
Distraction
Can I work undisturbed? Am I not distracted by phone, colleagues or other tasks?
💡 Tip: Complete the current task before starting something new. Mark your place when interrupted.
5. Lack of Teamwork
Lack of Teamwork
Am I working well with colleagues? Is there clear task division and support?
💡 Tip: Share knowledge and experiences. Offer help and ask for help when needed.
6. Fatigue
Fatigue
Have I slept enough? Do I feel physically and mentally fit for this task?
💡 Tip: Take regular breaks. Report fatigue to your supervisor. Vary tasks.
7. Lack of Resources
Lack of Resources
Do I have the right tools, materials and sufficient time to perform the task safely?
💡 Tip: Never use improvised tools. Report shortages in advance.
8. Pressure
Time / Work Pressure
Am I feeling pressure to work fast? Can I perform the task safely within the given time?
💡 Tip: Discuss unrealistic deadlines with your supervisor. Safety before speed.
9. Lack of Assertiveness
Lack of Assertiveness
Do I dare to say no or voice doubts? Do I speak up in unsafe situations?
💡 Tip: It's OK to say STOP. Address colleagues about unsafe behavior.
10. Stress
Stress
Do I have personal or work-related stress affecting my concentration?
💡 Tip: Talk about it with a colleague or supervisor. Take a step back if needed.
11. Lack of Awareness
Lack of Awareness
Do I have oversight of the situation? Do I see the bigger picture or have tunnel vision?
💡 Tip: Regularly take a step back. Ask for a second opinion in complex situations.
12. Norms
Norm Erosion
Am I following procedures or taking shortcuts because "we always do it this way"?
💡 Tip: Deviating from procedures = extra risk. Report impractical procedures so they can be improved.

Safety Helmet
▼
✓ Proper Use
  • Always wear the helmet with the brim facing forward
  • Adjust the headband so the helmet sits securely but doesn't pinch
  • The helmet should sit 2-3 cm above the eyebrows
  • Use the chin strap if present, especially when working at height or in wind
  • Do not wear a cap or headgear under the helmet — this reduces protection
⚠️ Do Not
Never drill holes in the helmet for ventilation — this weakens the structure
Do not use as storage or seating
Do not wear helmet backwards (unless specifically designed for this)
🔄 Maintenance & Inspection
  • Clean with lukewarm water and mild soap — no solvents
  • Check daily for cracks, dents and discoloration
  • Replace immediately after a hard impact, even without visible damage
  • Maximum lifespan: 3-5 years from production date (check inside)
  • Replace inner lining if hardened, cracked or soiled
💡Do not store the helmet in direct sunlight or at high temperatures — UV and heat age the material faster.
🥽
Safety Glasses
▼
✓ Proper Use
  • Put on glasses with clean, dry hands — not with work gloves on
  • Glasses should fit snugly around the eyes without gaps
  • For spectacle wearers: use over-glasses or prescription safety glasses
  • Choose the right type: clear for indoors, tinted for outdoors, splash-proof for liquids
⚠️ Do Not
Never put on/take off with dirty or chemically contaminated gloves — this brings substances to your face
Do not wear on forehead or around neck when not in use
Do not continue using scratched lenses — reduces vision and protection
🔄 Maintenance
  • Clean daily with water and soft cloth or special lens wipe
  • Use anti-fog spray for fogging problems
  • Store in protective case or pouch to prevent scratches
  • Replace when scratched, cracked or deformed
💡Glasses fogging up? Ensure good ventilation, use anti-fog coating, or choose a model with ventilation openings.
😎
Goggles & Face Shield
▼
✓ When to Use What
  • Goggles: For chemicals, splash hazard, dust — fully seal around eyes
  • Face shield: Extra protection for entire face — always IN COMBINATION with safety glasses
  • When grinding/turning: face shield protects against flying particles
⚠️ Important Warnings
Face shield alone is NOT sufficient — always wear safety glasses underneath
Check that goggles are suitable for the specific chemicals
💡For chemical work: wear gloves UNDER the sleeves of your jacket, so liquid doesn't run into the glove when splashed.
🎧
Hearing Protection
▼
✓ Types & Use
  • Earplugs (disposable): Roll up, pull ear up-back, insert and hold until expanded
  • Custom molded plugs: Insert with twisting motion, check seal
  • Earmuffs: Place fully over ears, ensure hair is not under the seal
📊 When Required?
Noise LevelAction
80-85 dB(A)Hearing protection must be available
>85 dB(A)Hearing protection mandatory
>87 dB(A)Never permitted (max. 87 dB with protection)
⚠️ Do Not
Do not insert earplugs with dirty hands — infection risk
Do not reuse disposable earplugs
Do not wear earmuffs over a beanie or hood — reduces attenuation
🔄 Maintenance
  • Custom plugs: clean daily with water and soap, disinfect weekly
  • Earmuffs: clean cushions with damp cloth, replace cushions annually
  • Store in clean box or pouch
💡Test the seal: if your own voice sounds 'hollow' when you speak, the earplugs are fitted correctly.
😷
Respiratory Protection
▼
✓ Types & Application
  • FFP1: Coarse dust, non-toxic (4x limit value)
  • FFP2: Fine dust, mist, smoke (10x limit value)
  • FFP3: Very fine dust, asbestos, biological agents (30x limit value)
  • Half-mask + filter: For gases/vapors, choose correct filter type
  • SCBA/Supplied air: For oxygen deficiency or highly toxic substances
✓ Proper Fitting
  • Shape the nose clip before putting on the mask
  • Cover nose, mouth AND chin completely
  • Pull straps over head — top strap above ears, bottom strap at neck
  • Fit-check: Cover mask with hands, inhale — mask should pull toward face
⚠️ Important Limitations
Dust masks (FFP) do NOT work against gases and vapors
No facial hair allowed in the sealing area — this breaks the seal
Do not use when oxygen content is below 19.5%
🔄 Replacement & Lifespan
TypeReplace
FFP disposableAfter 1 shift or when moisture accumulates
FFP with 'R' (reusable)Max. 1 week or when resistance increases
Gas filters (A, B, E, K)At odor/taste breakthrough or max. 6 months after opening
Particle filters (P2, P3)When breathing resistance increases or visibly saturated
💡Always store gas filters in airtight packaging after use — they also absorb from ambient air.
🧤
Work Gloves (Mechanical)
▼
✓ Proper Selection
  • Leather gloves: General work, handling, moderate heat
  • Cut-resistant gloves: Working with sharp materials (note cut class A1-F)
  • Nitrile coated: Grip with oily materials
  • Impact resistant: For pinch hazard, hammering, handling heavy objects
⚠️ Do Not
No gloves with rotating machinery — entanglement hazard!
Work gloves are NOT suitable for chemicals
Do not continue wearing wet gloves — less grip and cold
🔄 Maintenance
  • Check daily for holes, wear and hardening
  • Leather gloves: oil occasionally to keep supple
  • Washable gloves: wash according to instructions, not in dryer
  • Replace when fingertips or palm are worn through
💡The right size is crucial — gloves too large reduce grip and feel, gloves too small fatigue your hands.
🧪
Chemical Resistant Gloves
▼
✓ Material Selection by Substance
MaterialSuitable for
NitrileOils, greases, many solvents
ButylKetones, esters, acids
NeopreneAcids, bases, alcohols
PVAAromatic solvents
VitonChlorinated solvents
ℹ️ALWAYS consult the Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for the specific substance!
⏱️ Breakthrough Time (Permeation)
  • Breakthrough time: The time until the chemical penetrates through the glove
  • This is NOT noticeable — the glove still looks fine
  • Class 1: >10 min | Class 6: >480 min
  • Rule of thumb: Change well before breakthrough time, max. 50-80% of stated time
⚠️ Critical Warnings
Once used with chemicals = not reusable for the same substance (permeation continues)
Double-layering does NOT double breakthrough time
Small holes are not visible but dangerous — do leak test
✓ Putting On and Removing
  • Put on gloves with clean, dry hands
  • Pull cuff OVER sleeves (otherwise liquids run into the glove)
  • Removing: Grip at cuff, pull off turning inside out — do not touch outside
  • Wash hands immediately after removing, even if you wore gloves
💡Leak test: fill glove with air (not with mouth!), pinch closed and squeeze — air bubbles = leak.
👢
Safety Footwear
▼
✓ Classification
ClassProperties
S1Toe 200J, antistatic, heel energy absorption
S1PS1 + puncture-resistant sole
S2S1 + water-resistant upper
S3S2 + puncture-resistant sole
S4/S5Fully waterproof boots
✓ Proper Use
  • Always wear with proper (safety) socks — never barefoot
  • Tie laces completely — loose laces are a trip hazard
  • Break in shoes before wearing them all day
  • Wear the correct class for the work activities
⚠️ Do Not
Do not use with crushed/damaged toe cap — reduced protection
Do not dry on heater or in sun — damages the leather
Do not continue wearing with wet feet — blisters and fungus risk
🔄 Maintenance
  • Clean regularly with brush and lukewarm water
  • Leather shoes: regularly apply oil or spray
  • Let wet shoes dry slowly (newspaper inside)
  • Check soles regularly for wear and tread
  • Replace when soles are worn through, toe cap damaged or laces broken
💡Have sweaty feet? Alternate shoes daily so they can dry out, or use insoles.
👔
Coveralls & Workwear
▼
✓ Types
  • Standard coverall: Protection against dirt, light mechanical risks
  • Antistatic (ESD): Mandatory in Ex-zones, prevents static discharge
  • Flame retardant (FR): For fire hazard, hot work, electrical work
  • Chemical resistant: Type 3-6 depending on exposure risk
  • Hi-Vis: With reflective strips for visibility
⚠️ Critical Points
Antistatic clothing: close ALL zips and buttons — open clothing is not effective
FR clothing: NEVER wash with fabric softener — this reduces flame retardancy
Do not wear synthetic clothing UNDER FR clothing — can melt with heat
🔄 Maintenance
  • Wash according to instructions on label — especially FR clothing
  • FR clothing not dry clean unless approved
  • Check reflective strips — replace when worn or peeling
  • Repair tears immediately — reduces protection
💡Antistatic clothing only works if the entire chain is correct: shoes and gloves must also be antistatic, and you must be grounded.
☣️
Chemical Coverall
▼
✓ Type Classification
TypeProtection against
Type 6Light splashes and mist
Type 5Dry particles and dust
Type 4Saturated splashes (jet spray)
Type 3Liquids under pressure
Type 1/2Gas-tight (with breathing air)
✓ Proper Donning
  • Put on OVER your workwear, not directly on skin
  • Close all zips completely, tape if necessary
  • Gloves OVER sleeves, tape if necessary
  • Boots/overshoes UNDER legs, tape closed
⚠️ Critical Warnings
Disposable = single use only — never reuse after chemical contact
Replace immediately when torn — protection is breached
Watch for overheating — chemical suits do not ventilate
✓ Safe Removal
  • Work from top to bottom, turn inside out
  • Do not touch outside with bare hands
  • Have someone help with type 3 or higher
  • Dispose as chemical waste
💡Plan work in chemical suit: max. 30-60 min continuous work, take breaks in clean environment to prevent overheating.
🪢
Fall Protection & Harness
▼
✓ Components
  • Harness: Distributes fall forces across body, ALWAYS with dorsal attachment point
  • Fall arrest lanyard: Arrests fall, max. fall distance ~2m + extension
  • Positioning lanyard: Keeps you in position, NOT for fall protection
  • Anchor point: Min. 12 kN, above you and as vertical as possible
✓ Donning Harness
  • Hold harness by shoulder straps, shake out
  • Step into leg loops, pull up like a backpack
  • Fasten chest strap at chest height (not too high/low)
  • Tighten leg straps — max. a flat hand should fit between
  • Check: all buckles closed, straps not twisted, D-ring centered on back
⚠️ Critical Warnings
After a fall: immediately condemn harness AND lanyard — invisible damage
Never attach to structures not approved as anchor points
Account for fall distance: anchor point height min. 6m above obstacles
Account for swing fall effect when moving horizontally
🔄 Inspection & Maintenance
  • Annual inspection by competent person mandatory
  • Before each use: visual inspection for wear, tears, corrosion
  • Store dry, out of sunlight, hang up — not in a knot
  • Do not expose to chemicals, oils or sharp objects
  • Max. lifespan: 10 years from manufacture (check label), regardless of use
💡Make a rescue plan BEFORE working at height — someone hanging in a harness after a fall must be rescued within 15 minutes (suspension trauma).

API Gravity Formula
°API = (141.5 / SG @ 60°F) - 131.5

SG @ 60°F = 141.5 / (°API + 131.5)

Density (kg/m³) = SG × 999.016
📊
API Gravity Scale
< 10°API — Heavier than water (sinks)
10°API — Equal to water (SG = 1.0)
> 10°API — Lighter than water (floats)

Classification:
• Extra Heavy: < 10°API
• Heavy: 10 - 22.3°API
• Medium: 22.3 - 31.1°API
• Light: 31.1 - 39°API
• Extra Light/Condensate: > 39°API

📐
VCF Formula (API/ASTM)
VCF = exp[-K₀ × ΔT × (1 + 0.8 × K₀ × ΔT)]

Where:
ΔT = T_obs - T_ref (°C)
K₀ = K₀ + K₁/ρ₁₅ + K₂/ρ₁₅² (table values)

Volume @ 15°C = Volume @ T × VCF
Density @ T = Density @ 15°C × VCF
